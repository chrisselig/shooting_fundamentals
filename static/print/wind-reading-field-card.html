Reading Wind
Without a Meter

Mirage, environment, and bracketing — three reads that get you to a hold before the timer starts.

01

The Mirage Visual Scale

Optic

Look through your rifle scope or spotting scope and intentionally focus slightly short of the target to see the heat waves in the air.

0MPH
Boil
90° vertical
 Waves rise straight up like steam. No horizontal wind component.
1–3MPH
Slow Drift
60° tilt
 Waves tilt diagonally at roughly a 60-degree angle.
4–7MPH
Medium Cross
30° tilt
 Waves tilt hard, running across the target frame at roughly a 30-degree angle.
8+MPH
Fast Wind
0° flat
 The mirage floats completely flat horizontally.
Flat is the ceiling. Once mirage goes flat it cannot tell you the difference between 8 mph and 15 mph — switch to grass or flags at this point.
02

The Mirage Zoom Sweet Spot

Glass Setup
12–18MAGNIFICATION Stay in this band. It's the ideal range for seeing the wavy lines of mirage clearly at 100 to 200 yards while keeping enough field of view to transition between targets on a stage.
−20%PARALLAX De-focus slightly. Turn your side focus knob slightly off-target — for a 100-yard target, dial to roughly 75 or 80 yards. The target goes slightly soft, but the air currents right in front of it pop out vividly.
03

The Environment as a Speed Gauge

Naked Eye

If the match director hasn't placed wind flags along the range, use these natural baselines to estimate speed.

2–3MPH Wind is faintly felt on your face; leaves on trees may twitch slightly.
4–6MPH Small twigs and light grass move continuously. Match flags stand out at roughly a 45-degree angle.
8–12MPH Whole branches of bushes shake, loose dust and paper lift off the ground, and flags blow straight out horizontally.
04

Build a Wind Bracket Before Your Stage

Squad Line

Never try to guess the exact mile-per-hour number while your match timer is ticking. Establish a bracket while waiting in the squad line.

  1. Observe the flags for 2 minutes before your turn.
  2. Identify the lull — the lowest the wind drops, e.g. 4 mph — and the gust — the highest it kicks up, e.g. 10 mph.
  3. Find your holds for those two exact speeds on your Cole-TAC Cheat Sheet card.
  4. Write both holds down before you build your position.
