Kestrel 5700 Elite

A handheld weather meter with Applied Ballistics' solver built in. It doesn't replace the reads on Reading Conditions — it's faster once you already understand what those reads are telling you.

01

5700 Elite vs. 5700X Elite

Two current models, same core sensors. Check which one you actually have before you go looking for a feature — the difference matters for one specific thing.

  • Kestrel 5700 Elite — the base model. Runs the full Applied Ballistics solver on-device out of the box.
  • Kestrel 5700X Elite — same sensors, faster processor, adjustable Bluetooth strength. Per Kestrel's own FAQ, only the 5700X can add WEZ (Weapon Employment Zone) analysis as a paid unlock — the base 5700 Elite doesn't have the processing headroom for it and can't be upgraded to add it later.

For NRL22 at typical match distances, WEZ analysis (a long-range hit-probability tool) isn't the feature that matters day to day — the wind/environment reading and solver underneath both models are the same.

02

What It Actually Measures

  • Wind speed & direction
  • Temperature, humidity, heat index
  • Pressure, and pressure/density altitude

All of that feeds Applied Ballistics' solver on the device itself, so it can output an actual firing solution (drop and wind hold), not just raw weather numbers you'd have to plug into something else.

Neither Kestrel nor Applied Ballistics markets this toward rimfire specifically It's pitched generically at long-range shooters. The framing on this page — that wind and muzzle velocity accuracy matter a lot for 22LR — is this site's own editorial take, not a manufacturer claim.
03

Muzzle Velocity Calibration

Worth Doing

The 5700 Elite line includes a built-in muzzle velocity calibration guide, which lets you true up your rifle's actual MV without owning a separate chronograph.

This matters more for 22LR than it might seem. Rimfire drop is steep and its trajectory is disproportionately sensitive to small MV errors compared to a fast centerfire round — a wrong MV assumption throws your dope off at exactly the distances NRL22 targets sit at. Run the calibration guide, and re-run it if you change ammo lots.

04

Using It At The Line

Conceptual Workflow

Exact menu screens change between firmware versions, so this is the shape of the workflow rather than a tap-by-tap guide — check your unit's current menu if a step doesn't match.

  1. Power on and let it settle for a real-time reading rather than reading it the instant you turn it on.
  2. Hold it so the wind vane/impeller is actually exposed to the wind, not blocked by your body or the barricade.
  3. Watch the wind reading over the same 1–2 minutes you'd use to build a bracket by eye (see Reading Conditions → Wind Bracket) — the meter is a faster, more precise version of that same lull/gust observation, not a replacement for watching the conditions.
  4. With a profile set up (see AB Quantum), the solver gives you a hold directly from the current reading.
05

Which App Does What

Two different apps pair with the device over Bluetooth, for two different jobs — easy to mix up when you're new to it.

  • Kestrel LiNK Ballistics (KLB) — firmware updates and device settings, like renaming the unit.
  • AB Quantum — your rifle/ammo profile and the actual firing solution, pulling live weather from the Kestrel once paired.

A USB LiNK dongle is also sold separately if you'd rather tether to a computer than pair over Bluetooth.
