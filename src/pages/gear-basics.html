Section 05

Gear Basics

Marketing wants you to buy the whole catalog on week one. Fundamentals don't need most of it — a rifle, glass, a bag, and reps will out-shoot a cart full of accessories in untrained hands.

Spend on reps before you spend on gear A cheaper setup with 2,000 rounds behind it beats a top-tier setup with 200. Ammo and range time are the actual budget line worth prioritizing early.
Actually Need
  • A 22LR rifle in decent mechanical condition, with a stock or chassis that lets you get behind it consistently
  • A scope you can see mirage through clearly (see Reading Conditions → Zoom Sweet Spot) — doesn't need to be top-shelf
  • A rear squeeze bag, and something to rest the front on — see Bags & Bipods for matching bags/bipod to props
  • Eye and ear protection — see Safety
  • Ammo that's consistent lot-to-lot for your rifle — consistency matters more than brand prestige at 22LR velocities
  • A way to keep score of your dope: notebook, app, whatever you'll actually maintain — a battery-free backup like a Mildot Master is worth having too
Marketed To Beginners
  • The most expensive scope on the shelf before you can see mirage well with a mid-tier one
  • A full prop/barricade collection before you've built a position off a chair, a box, or a fence post
  • Match-grade everything, before your trigger control is the limiting factor rather than the rifle
  • A wind meter (see Kestrel 5700) before you can read mirage and environment without one — the meter is faster once you already understand what it's telling you, not a replacement for the reads in Reading Conditions

A note on ammo

22LR ammunition performance varies noticeably by lot, even within the same product line. Test a few brands/lots for what your specific rifle groups best with rather than assuming the most expensive match ammo will automatically outperform — it often does, but not always, and not in every rifle.

No reloading data here This site won't publish load recipes, powder charges, or pressure data — that's genuinely dangerous to get wrong from a secondhand source, and 22LR is factory-loaded rimfire ammunition anyway (not something handloaded at home). If you're shooting centerfire elsewhere and want load data, go to a published manual from a component manufacturer, not a website summary.
