AB Quantum

Applied Ballistics' current ballistic solver app — listed as "AB Quantum" on the App Store and "Applied Ballistics Quantum" on Google Play, same app. It replaced the older Applied Ballistics Mobile app.

01

What It Does

It's a ballistic solver built on Applied Ballistics' Doppler-radar-derived drag models — available on iOS and Android.

  • Builds a rifle/ammo profile: muzzle velocity, ballistic coefficient/drag model, zero, scope height, twist rate.
  • Produces a firing solution — drop and wind holds — from that profile plus current conditions.
  • Supports truing your profile against real impacts, so the solver gets more accurate the more you shoot and correct it.
  • Can log data directly from a Bluetooth chronograph (e.g. an Optex SpeedTracker) into a profile, which is the more precise way to nail down your actual MV.
02

Free vs. Elite

There are two tiers — Ultralite (free) and Elite (paid). Pricing isn't listed here since it's the kind of thing that changes; check current pricing in the app store listing.

Elite unlocks automatically if you're paired to an Elite-enabled Kestrel Owning a Kestrel 5700 or 5700X Elite unlocks Elite features in the app at no extra cost while it's actively connected. Some users have reported it reverting to a paywall prompt when the Kestrel isn't paired — if you see that, it's likely just the connection dropping, not a purchase being lost.
03

Building A Profile

Do This Before A Match

The app is only useful once a profile exists for the exact rifle and ammo combination you're shooting. It's one Profile Edit screen rather than a click-through wizard — these are the field groups, top to bottom, in the order they matter:

  1. Muzzle velocity. Use a chronograph if you have one, or the Kestrel's built-in MV Cal Guide (see Kestrel 5700 Elite) if you don't. Rimfire drop is unusually sensitive to MV error, so this is worth getting right before anything else.
  2. Bullet & drag model from AB's library where your exact bullet is listed, or a custom entry if it isn't — see If Your Bullet Isn't Listed below. G7 fits boat-tail rifle bullets better than G1 for most modern match bullets; CDM (radar-derived, built by Applied Ballistics themselves) is the most accurate option where it exists, but it only covers bullets they've already tested — you can't generate one yourself for a bullet outside their library.
  3. Zero range and sight height/twist — the conditions you actually zeroed at, and your scope height over bore and barrel twist direction, which the app uses to calculate a stability factor for the bullet.
  4. Scope units and reticle — match this to what's actually in the scope, since it drives how the solution screen displays your hold.
  5. True it once you have real impacts at distance — see Truing below, rather than trusting the math alone.

Exact field labels and screen layout can shift between app updates, so this is deliberately described at the level of "what you need to enter and roughly where," not a pixel-accurate walkthrough — the app's own in-app help will match your current version better than a diagram on this page would.

If Your Bullet Isn't Listed

.22LR loads are exactly the kind of bullet that can come up empty in a library search — here's the fallback order, straight from Applied Ballistics' own guidance where it exists.

  1. Search again before assuming it's missing. Check for the bullet under a slightly different manufacturer or product name — library search misses are often a naming mismatch, not an actual gap.
  2. Use Add Ammo for a custom entry. This lets you pick a drag model — G1, G7, or Custom — and enter the matching ballistic coefficient and bullet weight yourself.
  3. G1 for flat-base bullets, G7 for boat-tail. Applied Ballistics' own FAQ uses .22LR as its example: use G1 for a flat-base bullet like a standard 22LR or a flat-base varmint bullet, G7 for a boat-tail design. Use the manufacturer's published BC for that drag model if they list one.
  4. Custom Drag Model (CDM) isn't something you can build. CDMs are derived from Applied Ballistics' own Doppler radar testing and only exist for bullets they've already measured. If your bullet isn't in the library, CDM isn't an option for it — G1 or G7 with a manufacturer BC is the real fallback, not a lesser one.
  5. Double-check the drag model and BC actually match after you change either one. AB's own FAQ flags this as a common mistake — switching the drag-model selector without the BC value updating to match leaves you with a mismatched pair that won't solve correctly.
Treat the entered BC as a starting point, not a fact Generic G1/G7 models fit .22LR's flat-base, heel-type shape loosely to begin with, and rimfire BC isn't constant — it decays as the bullet slows through the transonic zone, sometimes dropping by a quarter or more from its supersonic value over a normal NRL22 string of distances. A manufacturer-published BC gets you a usable starting profile, but DSF Calibration — which specifically targets that transonic falloff — is doing more real work for a rimfire profile than it would for a centerfire one built on a well-fit G7.
04

The Solution Screen

Once a profile exists, this is what you're reading at the line — a range input, two solved outputs, and more detail available if you tap in.

Range card and target card are separate views built from the same profile — they add energy, time of flight, and Mach number at each range, with transonic ranges (where the bullet is slowing through the speed of sound and gets less predictable) flagged so you know which rows to trust less.

05

Truing

Two Separate Tools

"Truing" isn't one button — it's two different calibration tools depending on where your bullet is in its flight.

  • MV Calibration — for supersonic ranges, generally 600yd and beyond (or shorter, for subsonic/rimfire loads where the bullet is already slow). After a shot, you enter the true observed drop — not the error, the actual value. If the app predicted a 5.25 mil hold but your real impact was at 5.5 mils, you enter 5.5 and apply it; the app back-solves an adjusted MV from that.
  • DSF Calibration — for the transonic zone, where the bullet is slowing through the speed of sound and standard drag models get less reliable. This only runs from the live-fire Shoot submenu, takes up to six calibration points, and is applied separately with its own "Use DSF" step.
Enter the real number, not a correction The easiest mistake here is entering how far off you were instead of where you actually landed. MV Cal wants the true observed drop value, full stop — treat it like reporting a fact, not doing the math for the app.
06

Pairing With A Kestrel

Once paired over Bluetooth to a Kestrel 5700 or 5700X Elite, the app pulls live wind and environmental data straight from the device instead of you entering it by hand, and profiles can transfer between the app and the device. Full step-by-step pairing (both sides) is on the Kestrel 5700 Elite page — short version: turn on Bluetooth on the Kestrel, set it to PC/Mobile mode, then find and tap it in AB Quantum's Devices screen.

If a reading looks frozen or stale There are scattered reports of the connection appearing to stick on an old reading after pairing. Try unpairing and re-pairing rather than trusting a value that hasn't moved in a while — and cross-check against what you're actually seeing in the wind (Reading Conditions) if something feels off.
