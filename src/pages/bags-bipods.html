Bags & Bipods

Which support to reach for on which prop, built around your actual kit — a flat bag that clamps to your rifle's ARCA rail, and your OG (original-size) Game Changer — plus whether a bipod is worth adding. 01

Bag, Bipod, Or Both

Your rail-mounted flat bag on the front and the OG Game Changer as a rear bag already cover most NRL22 props without a bipod in the mix at all. The question isn't really "bag or bipod" for you — it's whether a given prop is one where your current two-bag setup is enough, or one where a bipod would genuinely add something.

  • Flat, wide, stable surface (a barricade top, a table-like prop) — your rail bag can sit and slide on this just as well as a bipod would lock onto it. A bipod's edge here is mostly in very flat, very wide setups where you want zero give and don't need to slide/track — otherwise your existing kit does the job.
  • Narrow edge, angled surface, or anything a bipod's feet can't seat flat on — this is where your rail bag actually beats a bipod outright: it conforms to the edge instead of needing a flat platform, and it's already attached to the rifle so there's nothing extra to place.
  • No real surface at all — you're bracing off your own body against a wall, post, or port edge — the OG Game Changer as a rear bag, or wedged between your body and the rifle, does this job. A bipod isn't useful here regardless of the prop.
  • Time pressure matters as much as theoretical stability. Stages are often shot cold with a short par time — a rail bag that's always attached to the rifle is inherently faster to get into position than digging a bipod out and deploying its legs, which is a real point in favor of sticking with your current kit rather than adding one.
Where a bipod would still earn its spot in your bag Mostly on the least NRL22-typical props: a genuinely flat, wide, open surface (ground prone off a table-like prop, a wide flat rooftop) where you want to free your support hand or track a moving target smoothly. If most of what you shoot is barricades, ports, and irregular props, your rail bag + OG Game Changer combo is arguably the higher-value setup to master before adding a bipod.
02

Your Bag Kit

Armageddon Gear

Two bags, two different jobs — front support attached to the rifle, and a rear bag that stays free so you can squeeze it.

  • Rail-mounted flat bag (front) — clamps directly to your rifle's ARCA rail, so it moves with the rifle instead of needing to be placed and re-placed on the prop. Because it's attached, it can slide fore-aft along the rail to fine-tune your contact point, and it conforms to edges and angled surfaces a bipod's feet would fight. This is your default front support on most NRL22 props.
  • OG Game Changer (rear) — the original-size Game Changer, used as a rear squeeze bag under the stock or grip. Flat top and side handle make it fast to plant, and squeezing it is how you make small elevation adjustments without touching the rifle's actual controls.

Together these cover front support (attached, edge-conforming) and rear support (free, squeezable) — which is most of what a bag setup needs to do. A bipod would add a third role: a rigid, hands-free front platform on flat ground-level or table-like props, which neither of your current bags is built to be.

Bag fill settles over time A bag that felt right when new can feel softer and less supportive a year of hard use later. If your OG Game Changer starts feeling mushy under the rifle, that's the fill compacting, not you losing skill — most bags can be topped up rather than replaced.

04

Building Height Into The Position

Reasoning, Not A Sourced Rule

There isn't good published guidance tying shooter height to specific bag stack heights or prop setups — this section is this site's own reasoning, written for a shooter around 5'8" (~172cm), with general notes for other heights. Treat it as a starting point to test, not a rule to follow blindly.

  • Around 5'8" — a mid-size primary bag (like a Shmedium Game Changer) under the rifle at a barricade port around chest-to-shoulder height should let you get bone-on-prop contact without excessive crouching or standing on toes. If a port feels awkwardly low or high, that's a real signal to adjust foot position and stance width before assuming you need different gear.
  • Taller shooters generally need less bag height to reach the same port, and may find themselves folding more at the knees or hips on low ports to get behind the rifle rather than over it.
  • Shorter shooters generally need more stack under the rifle at the same port, and may want a firmer, taller primary bag (or a bag stacked on a low box/prop, where the stage allows it) rather than trying to add height with muscle tension alone.
  • The one principle that holds regardless of height: read the prop's actual contact points and adapt your position to them, rather than forcing a position that worked on a different prop or that you saw someone else use. This is the same idea as Positional Shooting → Building A Position — height just changes where your body's natural contact points land relative to a given prop.
Don't force elbow-on-knee if your build doesn't fit the port If elbow-on-knee contact isn't reachable at a given height without straining, sliding your firing hand down the grip to lower your elbow, or resting the full forearm across the knee instead of just the elbow point, are both more repeatable than muscling a position that doesn't fit your proportions.
05

At The Line

A short mental checklist for the walkthrough, before your par time starts:

  1. What does the prop actually offer — flat and wide, narrow edge, curved, or nothing but your own body against it?
  2. Rail bag front, OG bag rear is your default — is this one of the rare flat/wide/ground-level props where a bipod (if you have one) would genuinely be better?
  3. Where's your bone-on-prop contact, and does it hold at your height without straining?
  4. Re-check NPA once you're built in — see Fundamentals → NPA.

Most of this is reps, same as everything else in Positional Shooting — the gear only helps once the position underneath it is sound.
